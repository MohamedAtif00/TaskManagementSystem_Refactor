## 1. Goal

**ATS exists so that educational curriculum content is produced through a consistent, automated pipeline — without anyone having to route work between teams by hand.**

The system’s job is to:

1. **Define the production process once** as a reusable workflow (stages, steps, responsible disciplines, expected durations, and allowed rework paths).
2. **Generate the work automatically** when a learning objective is created, and keep generating the next work as upstream work completes.
3. **Give every role the right view** of what to do next, what is blocked, and how a subject, sprint, or person is performing.
4. **Measure actual effort against estimates**, with a complete audit trail of every status change and every rework.
5. **Keep workforce availability in the same system** as production, so leave, permission, and work-from-home decisions sit next to the work they affect.

ATS is **not** a generic task tracker. Work items are generated by a workflow engine and are permanently bound to curriculum entities (learning objectives), not created as free-floating tickets.

## 2. Business context

### 2.1 The organisation

Selah El-Telmeez manufactures educational curriculum content. Every learning objective travels through a multi-discipline production line. Typical disciplines include:

Instructional Design, Subject Matter Expert review, Proofreading, Graphic Design, Voice-Over, Animation, Multimedia Development, Native Development, Quality Assurance, and Translation.

People are organised as:

| Structure | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Group** | A production discipline. Work belongs to a Group before it belongs to a person. |
| **Team** | An administrative reporting unit of people. |
| **Section** | An oversight layer spanning several Groups, headed by a Section Head. |
| **Team Leader relationship** | Direct-report link used for the first level of HR approval. |

Content is catalogued and then decomposed:

```
Academic Year  →  Curriculum Project  →  Term  →  Subject Group  →  Subject
                                                                      │
                                            Unit  →  Lesson  →  Learning Objective
                                                                      │
                                                              Tasks (generated)
```

The **Learning Objective is the unit of production**. A workflow is attached there, sprints are planned there, and effort and progress are measured there. A **Subject** is the level at which the business declares work complete: when no incomplete tasks remain in a subject, the subject closes automatically.

### 2.2 The problem before ATS

Production was coordinated informally. The organisation could not reliably answer:

- Which discipline currently owes work on which lesson?
- How much effort a content item has consumed against its estimate?
- Why a piece of work was sent back, and with what evidence?
- Whether a team has capacity next month, given approved leave and work-from-home?

Handoffs depended on individuals remembering to notify the next team. Rework had no audit trail. Workforce availability lived in a separate system, so production planning was blind to who would actually be at work.

### 2.3 The solution

Management defines each production process once as a **workflow schema** — a graph of stages, each containing ordered steps, each step bound to the discipline that performs it and to an expected duration. Attaching a schema to a learning objective causes the system to generate the required work and to keep generating downstream work as upstream work completes, respecting prerequisites.

Around that engine, ATS provides Kanban and spreadsheet execution, sprint planning, rollback with evidence, effort capture, productivity scoring, role-appropriate dashboards, and a full workforce-administration module (leave, permission, work-from-home) with multi-level approval.

### 2.4 Current state

The system is **in production use**. As of August 2026 it comprises 27 API controllers (~210 HTTP actions), about 50 page routes, 37 persisted `DbSet`s, and 3 hosted background services plus an in-memory presence timer. Core production, sprint, HR, notification, and reporting capabilities are delivered. Remaining investment is concentrated in security enforcement, operational correctness, and new capabilities such as capacity forecasting.

## 3. Target audience

ATS is an **internal system**. It is used by Selah El-Telmeez staff (and contracted contributors known to HR), not by students, schools, or the public.

| Audience | Who they are | What they need from ATS |
|---|---|---|
| **Production members** | Designers, writers, reviewers, developers, QA, translators | A clear backlog of work for their discipline; claim, work, pause, comment, flag, and request rework |
| **Team Leaders** | Heads of one discipline | Team throughput, flagged-task escalations, first-level HR approval, Team-Leader-designated steps |
| **Section Heads** | Oversight across several disciplines | Section-scoped workload and HR approval for users in their section’s groups |
| **Project Managers / Coordinators** | People who design the catalogue and the process | Curriculum structure, workflow schemas, staffing, sprint planning, and override powers (skip, jump, change process) |
| **Business Owner** | Accountable for delivery, workforce cost, and access | Organisation-wide visibility, final HR approval, session audit, force-logout, all-staff leave balances |
| **HR administration** | Entitlements and compliant approvals (via Owner / Project Manager) | Leave types, balances, approval chains, medical certificates, user change audit |

**Not an audience:** students, teachers consuming published content, payroll clerks, or external customers. ATS tracks that production work happened; it does not author content, deliver learning, or process payroll.

## 4. Scope

### 4.1 In scope (delivered)

| Area | What is included |
|---|---|
| Curriculum catalogue | Academic Year → Curriculum Project → Term → Subject Group → Subject; archive; delete-protection while descendants exist |
| Content decomposition | Unit → Lesson → Learning Objective; schema required on every learning objective |
| Workflow design | Reusable schemas, graph of stages, ordered steps, Task Bank, rollback targets, duplicate and archive |
| Automated work | Task generation on create and on completion; Team-Leader vs group-backlog routing; subject auto-close |
| Task execution | Backlog → To Do → Doing → Done; claim, pause, flag, comment, priority; Kanban and sheet views |
| Rework | Configured rollback paths with issue notes, **problem type**, clarification, and file evidence |
| Managerial intervention | Skip, jump, change process, complete on another user’s behalf — all journaled |
| Sprints | Time-boxed increments attached to learning objectives; cross-project boards |
| Dashboards | Role-based landing screens for five roles |
| Reporting | Daily operational report, Task Logger productivity scoring, project/sprint analytics (including LO progress), CSV/Excel export |
| Organisation admin | Users, groups, teams, sections; user change log |
| Workforce | Five leave types, four permission types, work-from-home; calendar + My Leaves; multi-level approval; monthly/annual resets |
| Manager workload | User Tasks screen (To Do / Doing per person) |
| Notifications | Real-time (SignalR) plus persistent inbox with inline approval |

### 4.2 Out of scope

| Excluded | Reason |
|---|---|
| Payroll and time-and-attendance | ATS records absence approvals only |
| Content authoring and asset storage | Artefacts live in each discipline’s production tools |
| Learning delivery (LMS) | ATS is a production system, not a student platform |
| Financials, budgeting, procurement | No cost or budget concepts in the model |
| Customer or partner portal | Internal staff only |
| Formal password or single sign-on | Currently a 6-character code (known gap) |
| Owner session audit / force-logout | Specified in earlier notes; not implemented |
| Native mobile applications | Web only |
| Multi-language UI | English only (Arabic content exists; UI is not localised) |
| Forward capacity forecasting | Required as a business objective; not yet built |
| Automatic ageing / stalled-work alerts | Escalation is currently manual via flagging |
| Estimate recalibration from observed effort | Task Logger compares actual vs expected; it does not update defaults |

### 4.3 Scope assumptions

- Users are internal staff or contracted contributors known to HR and issued a system code.
- The system operates inside the corporate network or behind the corporate reverse proxy (`ats.stp.local`).
- Content artefacts remain in each discipline’s tools; ATS references work, not files (except rollback evidence and medical certificates).

### Forward roadmap (from the BRD)

| Phase | Focus | Exit intent |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 0 — immediate** | Security remediation (authorisation, uploads, secrets, login lockout, token validation) | Independent security review: no function reachable without the right role; no employee document retrievable without entitlement |
| **Phase 1** | Operational correctness (session renewal, report name-exclusion bug, API URL, tests, release gates) | Daily use is not interrupted; reports are trustworthy |
| **Phase 2** | Business agility (configurable thresholds, dashboard sign-off, schema versioning, leave-balance history) | Policy can change without a software release |
| **Phase 3** | New capability (capacity forecast, ageing alerts, estimate recalibration, email/mobile approvals) | Planning answers “can we take this on, and when will it land?” |
| **Phase 4** | Platform (localisation, accessibility, horizontal scale, technical debt) | Fit for growth and Arabic-first staff |

There is no frozen end date for Phases 1–4. Phase 0 is the prerequisite to further feature investment.

## 7. Success criteria

Success is measured against the business objectives the system was built to serve.

| ID | Success criterion | How we know | Current |
|---|---|---|---|
| SC-01 | Pipeline work is generated by the system, not created by hand | 100% of schema-driven tasks originate from learning-objective creation or `CreateNext`; ad-hoc tasks are the exception | Met |
| SC-02 | Every learning objective follows a defined process | Schema selection is mandatory when creating a learning objective | Met |
| SC-03 | Production and rework are fully traceable | Every status change is journaled; every rework has notes, problem type, clarification, and optional files | Met |
| SC-04 | Actual effort is comparable to estimates | Work sessions record start/stop with an explicit end reason; Task Logger scores early / on time / late | Met |
| SC-05 | Each of the five roles lands on a relevant dashboard with scoped data | Role-specific landing API and UI | Met (widget sign-off still recommended) |
| SC-06 | Work can be planned in time-boxed increments | Learning objectives assignable to sprints; sprint boards and analytics exist | Met |
| SC-07 | Leave, permission, and work-from-home are processed end to end in ATS | Preview, submit, multi-level approval, balance enforcement, monthly/annual resets | Met |
| SC-08 | Managers act on requests without hunting for them | Real-time events plus persistent inbox with inline approve/reject | Met |
| SC-09 | System access is accountable | Every session has start, end, reason, IP, and device; Owner can browse and force logout | **Not met** |
| SC-10 | Production capacity can be forecast against confirmed availability | Forward view combining estimates, historical throughput, and approved absence | **Not met** |
| SC-11 | Stalled work is detected before it becomes a delivery risk | Automatic ageing alerts on duration or unclaimed dwell | **Not met** (flagging is manual) |
| SC-12 | Estimation accuracy improves over time | Observed durations feed back into default estimates | **Not met** |

### Release-quality bar (must remain true)

| Criterion | Target |
|---|---|
| Authorisation | Every function restricted to entitled roles **on the server**, not only in the UI |
| Confidentiality | Medical certificates and rework attachments retrievable only by entitled users |
| Effort integrity | Abandoned sessions do not accumulate phantom work time (inactivity watchdog) |
| Subject closure | A subject closes only when no incomplete tasks remain, and the Owner is notified |
| Audit | Task events, user-record changes, and sessions are attributable to a person and a time |

### What “done” for v1.0 means

v1.0 is **functionally complete** for the production pipeline, execution, sprints, HR, notifications, and reporting described in this brief. It is **not** done against corporate security or planning-maturity standards until Phase 0 and the unmet criteria SC-10–SC-12 are addressed.
